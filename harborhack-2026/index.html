For the first time, some of the collaborators aren't people, and they don't clock out when the sprint ends.

Persistent synthetic teammates are taking a new seat in engineering work—not as autocomplete, not as a search box, but as something closer to a colleague who remembers the project and shows up again tomorrow.

HarborHack is where developers explore what software development looks like when a persistent synthetic teammate becomes part of the team.

At HarborHack 2026, every team works alongside ROOK, a persistent synthetic engineering teammate, to find out how that collaboration works in practice: where it helps, where it doesn't, and how much still depends on human judgment.

This isn't a contest to see who can generate the most code.

It's a weekend to practice leading a team that includes a synthetic collaborator, and to see up close how the software development team is changing.

FAQ

Participant questions

Who can participate?

Eligibility is being finalized by organizers.

Do I need a team before arriving?

No. Individuals may register before forming a team.

Does ROOK count as a team member?

No. ROOK is used by every team but does not count toward the human team-size limit.

Must every team use ROOK?

Yes. Every HarborHack team receives the same persistent synthetic engineering teammate: ROOK.

May teams use Claude, Codex, Gemini, GPT-based tools, or other agents?

Yes. They may be used as specialist executors while ROOK remains the common coordination layer and humans make final decisions.

What qualifies for Challenge 2?

AI inference must run locally on team-controlled hardware with no cloud-hosted foundation-model inference or commercial pay-per-token AI inference APIs. Final requirements will be confirmed by organizers.

Can beginners use a local model?

Yes, but beginners should test a small fallback model before the event and ask mentors for guidance when mentor support is configured.

Can teams prepare before HarborHack?

They may prepare tools, learning, and setup. Core project work must occur during HarborHack unless final rules state otherwise.

Must source code be public?

Provide an accessible source repository whenever possible. If public access is not possible, follow final organizer instructions for judge access.

Is a submission video required?

No prerecorded project video is required.

How do live presentations work?

Every eligible team presents live using the configured presentation and Q&A durations.

What should participants bring?

Bring a laptop, charger, headphones, development tools, Git access, and any hardware needed for your project or local inference.

How do participants request mentor help?

Mentor support instructions are configuration-backed and currently coming soon.

Can teams enter both challenges?

Yes, if the project satisfies the requirements for both challenges.

What happens after HarborHack?

Teams are encouraged to continue the project and retain repository, documentation, backlog, mission, engineering history, and ROOK context where available.

Where does the actual ROOK workflow take place?

The engineering workflow occurs in the separate ROOK product, not inside the CharlestonHacks website. This page only orients participants and links to setup when configured.
